# ⚡ Chapter 7: Maritime Energy Management

---

## 7.1 Introduction

Maritime energy management focuses on **optimizing fuel consumption and reducing greenhouse gas emissions** from ships.

It is now a key requirement under IMO regulations.

---

## 7.2 Objectives

* Reduce fuel consumption
* Reduce CO₂ emissions
* Improve energy efficiency
* Comply with IMO regulations
* Support decarbonization of shipping

---

## 7.3 Key Energy Indicators

### ⚙️ EEDI (Energy Efficiency Design Index)

Measures energy efficiency at ship design stage.

### ⚙️ EEXI (Energy Efficiency Existing Ship Index)

Applies to existing ships to evaluate technical efficiency.

### ⚙️ CII (Carbon Intensity Indicator)

Rates operational efficiency of ships annually (A to E rating).

---

## 7.4 SEEMP (Ship Energy Efficiency Management Plan)

SEEMP is a mandatory plan including:

* Operational measures
* Monitoring procedures
* Energy efficiency improvements
* Data collection system

---

## 7.5 MRV and DCS

* MRV (Monitoring, Reporting, Verification) – EU system
* DCS (Data Collection System) – IMO system

They track fuel consumption and emissions.

---

## 7.6 ETS (Emission Trading System)

A carbon pricing mechanism applied in some regions (notably EU).

---

## 7.7 Operational Measures

* Speed optimization (slow steaming)
* Hull cleaning
* Propeller maintenance
* Voyage optimization
* Weather routing

---

## 7.8 Alternative Fuels

* LNG
* Methanol
* Ammonia
* Hydrogen
* Biofuels

---

## 7.9 Energy Saving Technologies

* Waste heat recovery
* Shaft generators
* Air lubrication systems
* Wind-assisted propulsion

---

## 7.10 Key Concept

> Energy management is now a core operational responsibility for all modern ships.
