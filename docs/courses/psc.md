# 🚨 Chapter 5: Port State Control (PSC)

---

## 5.1 Definition

Port State Control (PSC) is the inspection of foreign ships in national ports to verify compliance with international maritime conventions.

It ensures that substandard ships do not operate.

---

## 5.2 Objectives of PSC

* Ensure maritime safety
* Protect the marine environment
* Verify crew working conditions
* Eliminate substandard shipping

---

## 5.3 Legal basis

PSC is based on international conventions such as:

* SOLAS
* MARPOL
* STCW
* MLC 2006

---

## 5.4 Inspection process

### Step 1: Initial inspection

* Certificates check
* Visual inspection

### Step 2: Detailed inspection

If deficiencies are found:

* Machinery inspection
* Safety equipment check
* Crew competence verification

---

## 5.5 Deficiencies

A deficiency is any condition that does not comply with international rules.

Examples:

* Invalid certificates
* Oil leaks
* Fire safety issues
* Poor maintenance

---

## 5.6 Detention of ship

A ship can be detained if deficiencies are:

* Serious
* Dangerous for safety
* Environmental risk

---

## 5.7 Paris MoU

PSC in Europe and surrounding regions is coordinated by the **Paris Memorandum of Understanding (Paris MoU)**.

Ships are classified into:

* White list (good compliance)
* Grey list (average)
* Black list (high risk)

---

## 5.8 Relationship with Flag State and RO

* Flag State: legal authority
* RO: certification body
* PSC: external control authority in port

---

## 5.9 Key Concept

> PSC acts as a “quality control system” of the global maritime industry.
